Landlords: What You Can Claim When You File Rental Income with IRAS

Published 23 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Drafted with AI assistance · reviewed by Serene Lee, ERA Realty

Rented out your HDB flat, a room, or a condo this year? That rent goes into your income tax return next year. The good news: you're taxed on net rent, not gross. Here's what you can claim, what you can't, and what to sort out before 31 Dec.

When to Declare

  • Rent received 1 Jan – 31 Dec is declared in the following year’s income tax return (Year of Assessment).
  • Filing season: file on myTax Portal when IRAS opens filing each year. Check your IRAS notification for that year’s deadline.
  • On No-Filing Service? Rental income isn't pre-filled for you. You still need to log in and file to declare it by the deadline.
  • Jointly owned? Rent, expenses and any loss are split by each owner's legal share. Each owner declares their own portion.

The Formula

  • Taxable rent = Gross rent − Mortgage interest − Other rental expenses
  • For “other rental expenses”, you choose one of two methods (below).
  • Expenses only count if they were incurred solely to earn the rent and during the tenancy period.

Option 1: 15% Deemed Expenses (the Easy Way)

  • Claim a flat 15% of gross rent instead of adding up receipts.
  • Plus your actual mortgage interest on the loan used to buy that property.
  • IRAS pre-fills the 15% in the online form.
  • No need to keep receipts for the other expenses. Keep mortgage interest statements for 5 years.
  • Not available if: mortgage interest was your only expense, the property is held in a trust or partnership, or it's approved for non-residential use (e.g. childcare centre, dormitory).
  • All or nothing: if you rent out more than one home, the same method applies to all of them. No mixing 15% on one and actual on another.

Option 2: Actual Expenses (Can Be Higher)

You can claim these if you paid them during the tenancy:

  • Mortgage interest on the loan used to buy the rented property (interest only, not principal).
  • Property tax for the rental period (excluding late-payment penalties).
  • Condo maintenance fees (MCST charges), painting, pest control.
  • Repairs that restore the property to its original state, e.g. fixing a leaking pipe or a broken aircon.
  • Fire insurance premiums.
  • Getting a tenant: agent commission, advertising, legal fees and stamp duty on the lease. This includes your first tenant. Leases over 3 years don't qualify.
  • Replacing furniture or appliances like-for-like, and hiring furniture.
  • Utilities and Wi-Fi you pay for the tenant and don't get reimbursed for.
  • Third-party management fees. If paid to a relative, the fee must be at market rate.
  • Costs between tenants (repairs, insurance, upkeep, property tax), if you were actively looking for a new tenant.
  • Damage repairs paid for out of a forfeited deposit.

Keep invoices, the tenancy agreement and bank statements for 5 years.

What You Can't Claim

  • Principal loan repayments.
  • Bank late charges or finance fees.
  • Renovation, upgrades or additions, e.g. new window grilles, new kitchen, initial repairs when you first bought the place.
  • Depreciation of furniture, or buying furniture you didn't have before.
  • Utilities the tenant reimbursed you for.
  • Property tax arrears from a previous year, and late-payment penalties.
  • Costs of your own home (e.g. rent you pay elsewhere while your flat is rented out).

  • Condo owners with MCST fees and a new tenant that year often beat 15% with actual expenses.
  • HDB owners with low conservancy charges and no major repairs usually do better with 15%.
  • IRAS has a free Rental Calculator (Excel) on its website to compare both before you file.

If You Made a Loss

  • A rental loss can't be set against your salary, and can't be carried forward to next year.
  • It can offset rent from another property in the same year, if all your properties were let at market rent.

Year-End Checklist (Do This Before 31 Dec)

  1. Total up the year’s rent received, month by month. Note any vacant months.
  2. Download your bank's annual mortgage interest statement.
  3. Collect invoices: property tax, MCST, repairs, insurance, agent fees, stamp duty.
  4. Check the lease was stamped via myTax Portal. Stamping also notifies IRAS of the tenancy.
  5. Separate “repair” from “improvement”. Only repairs count.
  6. Run both methods through IRAS's Rental Calculator and pick the lower taxable figure.
  7. Joint owners: agree the split based on legal share before filing.

Watch-Outs

  • Don't estimate. IRAS takes fictitious or inflated expense claims seriously, with heavy penalties.
  • Renting out HDB? Tax is separate from HDB's rules. You still need HDB's approval and to meet MOP and minimum-stay rules before renting out a whole flat or a room.
  • Unsure about your situation (trust, company-owned, mixed use, overseas owner)? Speak to a tax professional.
